## How do you hold a value proposition on one landing page?

GetGloby translated and adapted marketing for teams in the U.S. and Europe. The proposition joined two models the market already read apart: the speed of an AI model, and a native linguist’s judgment. The landing page had to make them read as one flow. We built it in Webflow. I led that work with [Carelí Sapino](https://www.linkedin.com/in/careli-sapino/), a UI designer on the design team.

<Facts>
  <Fact title="Proposition">AI speed and human judgment, visible in the first viewport.</Fact>
  <Fact title="Evidence">Below the CTAs (primary and secondary), a screenshot of the platform. The landing does not run the product.</Fact>
  <Fact title="Scope">The landing explains the service. The workspace stays in the product.</Fact>
</Facts>

### Problem: two propositions, easy to split apart.

A visitor could read "AI translation" and assume full automation, or read "native speakers" and assume a slow service. The hierarchy had to hold both before the decision. The primary CTA, Get started for free, and the secondary, Book a demo, sit under one line: combine the speed of AI and the precision of native linguists.

### Opportunity: a scroll that follows the visitor’s question.

What it does. How AI steps in. Where human review enters. Which asset types it covers. Social proof, objections, and the same CTA pair at the close.

<Facts>
  <Fact title="AI, scoped">Review, glossaries, and tone notes, without promising the model publishes alone.</Fact>
  <Fact title="Human in the loop">Native speakers have their own section, with an entry point to request a review.</Fact>
  <Fact title="Work surface">Documents, ads, marketing assets, video, and captions, named on the page.</Fact>
</Facts>

## The page we published

Below the CTAs (primary and secondary), the hero shows a screenshot of the platform. It is product evidence, not a translator embedded in the landing. Then the logos, a short claim, Ship better and faster, and the rest of the scroll separates AI technology from native speakers, lists the assets, quotes a LATAM team, answers objections, and closes on the same proposition.

![GetGloby landing page, from the hero through the FAQ and close](/projects/getgloby/landing.webp#wide "Landing page: the AI and native-linguist proposition, from the hero through the FAQ.")

The character from [Jaga](/jaga) sits in the hero as a guide, not as the product. The identity case tells that story. On this page, the character only had to make the review feel human.

## What we left off the page

> The landing shows the proposition. It does not try to be the product.

We kept the screenshot below the CTAs (primary and secondary) and left the workspace, the team setup, and the rest of the app. A long product tour would have competed with the proposition. That product continues in [Marketfully](/marketfully).

## What I learned

<Facts>
  <Fact title="Evidence before explanation">A screenshot of the platform, below the CTAs (primary and secondary), did more than a paragraph about AI and people.</Fact>
  <Fact title="Separate the roles">AI and native speakers needed their own sections, or one of them lost hierarchy.</Fact>
</Facts>
