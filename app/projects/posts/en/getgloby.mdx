## How do you turn a services MVP into a product teams can run?

I joined GetGloby in 2022 as Head of Design and UX, the first product hire, reporting to the CTO and CPO. The product started as translation and localization services. The work ran with teams across the U.S. and Europe.

<Facts>
  <Fact title="A cohesive brand">The service needed a face teams could recognize.</Fact>
  <Fact title="A design system">Interactions had to stay intuitive as the product grew.</Fact>
  <Fact title="A shared backlog">Engineering, product, and design needed one list they could run.</Fact>
</Facts>

### Problem: an early MVP, not a product.

The platform was early. Teams could not yet run it as a product. I redesigned it from that MVP into something engineering, product, and design could share, and I built the design team from the ground up.

![The early GetGloby product, before the redesign](/projects/getgloby/mvp.webp#wide "Early MVP: translation and localization services, not yet a product teams could run.")

### Opportunity: marketing assets that travel across languages.

I led the design function for GetGloby’s translation and localization services. We kept interactions intuitive, kept a culture of user feedback, and looked at new ways to make marketing assets accessible across languages.

<Facts>
  <Fact title="AI and people">The marketing site put AI and native linguists on the same path.</Fact>
  <Fact title="Feedback">Usability came from a culture of user feedback, not from a single redesign.</Fact>
  <Fact title="A name for the motor">Jaga, the product identity for the transcreation engine, was part of the same work.</Fact>
</Facts>

[Jaga](/jaga) gave that engine a name teams could sell.

## After the acquisition

That foundation supported the acquisition by [MotionPoint](https://www.motionpoint.com/) and the move into [Marketfully](/marketfully), where GetGloby’s technology now lives inside Marketfully.AI.

![GetGloby’s technology moving into Marketfully.AI](/projects/getgloby/after-acquisition.webp#wide "After the acquisition: the same technology, inside a larger marketing group.")

I stayed through the path from MVP to acquisition, then into the platform as it became part of a larger marketing group. The product changed name and shape. The job did not: keep the experience clear enough that global teams could actually use it.

## What we were willing to trade

The product changed name and shape. We kept the experience clear instead of protecting the old form.

> The product changed name and shape. The job did not.

## What I learned

<Facts>
  <Fact title="Practice before polish">Standards, rituals, and a relationship with engineering outlast a single redesign.</Fact>
  <Fact title="Clarity survives the rename">Global teams can use the product only if the experience stays clear when the name and the shape change.</Fact>
</Facts>
