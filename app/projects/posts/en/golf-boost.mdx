## How do you coach a swing from a phone?

Golf Boost Software Company sat at the overlap of sport and software. I designed the iOS app, branding, and experience for a golf training product.

<Facts>
  <Fact title="The app">An iOS product that used body recognition to detect movements.</Fact>
  <Fact title="The feedback">People needed something they could follow, not a raw reading of the swing.</Fact>
  <Fact title="The brand">I led the rebranding and the visual and interaction foundations.</Fact>
</Facts>

### Problem: feedback people could follow.

I designed an iOS app that used body recognition to detect movements and give people feedback they could follow. User interviews shaped the interaction and usability decisions, so the interface followed how people actually trained.

### Opportunity: training that was not a local service.

This was part-time work, in projects rather than a continuous engagement. It ran in parallel with my full-time roles. I worked directly with the owner, who was based in Miami. The product was not a local service. It was a golf training app, built to be used beyond that city.

<Facts>
  <Fact title="Frame">The golfer had to fit in frame before the swing meant anything.</Fact>
  <Fact title="Capture">The swing was captured automatically, so training did not stop to operate the phone.</Fact>
  <Fact title="A first week">The first week was free, with unlimited swings, so people could feel the loop before they committed.</Fact>
</Facts>

## A brand and a system around the training

I led the rebranding of Golf Boost, tightening the visual identity and how people met the product. I also built the design system and the visual and interaction foundations so the product felt cohesive as the team kept building. The engagement stayed part-time. The system was what let the work continue between projects.

## What we were willing to trade

Part-time work, in projects, in parallel with full-time roles. The product still needed a system, or each project would restart the identity.

> The engagement was part-time. The product still had to feel like one product.
