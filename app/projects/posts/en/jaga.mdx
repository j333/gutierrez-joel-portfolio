## How do you name a capability nobody can see?

Jaga is [GetGloby](/getgloby)’s transcreation engine: advertising translation technology that uses AI and NLP to read tone, cultural context, and intent, not just words.

<Facts>
  <Fact title="In the product">The capability had to live in the product UI, not only in a brand book.</Fact>
  <Fact title="In market">Sales materials needed the same name the interface used.</Fact>
  <Fact title="One system">Screens and decks had to share a language, or the motor stayed two different stories.</Fact>
</Facts>

### Problem: the engine was powerful but invisible.

Marketing teams could not see it, name it, or explain it. The brief was to make advanced technology feel usable without diluting what the motor actually does. We ran an internal workshop (objective, proto-persona, logo exploration) to reach a positioning the team could defend. Not customer research. A shared starting point we could build on.

![Brainstorming boards for the Jaga logo, objective, and proto-persona research](/projects/jaga/identity-brief.webp#wide-animated "Workshop: objective, proto-persona, and logo exploration.")

![The motor before it had a name, set next to the identity that made it pointable](/projects/jaga/before-after.webp#wide "From an invisible engine to a capability teams could name.")

### Opportunity: one language for the product and the pitch.

Product screens and sales decks needed the same language. Otherwise the motor stayed an engineering story in one room and a pitch in another. Transcreation is cultural work, so the identity had to carry that weight and still sit in an interface without competing with the task.

<Facts>
  <Fact title="Name it">Teams could point at the capability instead of decorating it.</Fact>
  <Fact title="Carry the culture">The identity had to hold cultural weight and still fit the task on screen.</Fact>
  <Fact title="Scale">One system for product and market, built to keep growing.</Fact>
</Facts>

## A system teams could point to

Together we ran workshops, defined positioning, and shipped a system that could scale across product and market.

## A character, not a mascot

Jaga is the face of the transcreation motor. The character had to feel magical, powerful, and wise: a guide for marketing teams crossing languages and markets, not a mascot pasted on top of the product.

  <Fact title="One face, two surfaces">Style, personality, and behavior had to hold in UI states and in marketing at the same time.</Fact>
</Facts>
